Abstract

Multi-agent ergodic coverage seeks trajectories whose collective visitation statistics match a prescribed target distribution, but extending this principle to complex three-dimensional surfaces raises two coupled challenges, where the coverage metric must respect intrinsic surface geometry, and the team must achieve complementary coverage without excessive overlap. We present geodesic flow matching, a trajectory optimization framework for multi-agent ergodic coverage directly on embedded triangle-mesh surfaces. We formulate coverage using a Sinkhorn divergence with squared geodesic ground cost and derive its intrinsic reference flow in closed form as a cross-minus-self entropic barycentric projection through Riemannian logarithm maps. This avoids differentiating mesh potentials and yields a smooth tangent guidance field that can be tracked by general off-surface robot dynamics through a surface-output linear-quadratic problem. For robot teams, a single transport problem over the aggregate coverage measure provides the only inter-agent coupling, where spatial partitioning emerges directly from the objective, while control synthesis decouples into independent Riccati solves for each agent, requiring neither task assignment nor Voronoi construction. Experiments on 18 meshes, including complex architectural surfaces, demonstrate robust multi-agent coverage. While other baseline methods remain competitive for uniform targets, their errors increase substantially for concentrated and sharp-support targets. These results establish our method as a geometry-aware and naturally team-compatible foundation for ergodic coverage on complex surfaces.
